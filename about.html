About

A career built at the intersection of public service, private enterprise, and place.

Few people have served at the federal level, led marketing for a major regional company, run their own business, and stayed deeply rooted in the community that raised them. Kristie Allen is one of them.

Public service

Earlier in her career, Kristie served as a presidential appointee in the George W. Bush administration. The appointment grounded her in how federal, state, and local government intersect, a perspective she brings to every civic engagement.

Marketing leadership for H&H Homes and Coldwell Banker Advantage

Kristie served as Senior Vice President of Marketing for H&H Homes and Coldwell Banker Advantage, the two companies owned by Ralph Huff and headquartered in Fayetteville. The role covered brand, advertising, and demand generation across both a homebuilding operation spanning the Carolinas and a regional real estate brokerage.

The work meant marketing a product that had to carry people through the single largest transaction of their lives, in markets as different as Fort Bragg military families, coastal retirees, and first-time Raleigh buyers. It taught her that brand and operations are inseparable: a promise made in advertising has to be delivered on a jobsite.

Entrepreneur and operator

Today Kristie owns and operates Two Men and a Truck of Fayetteville and Sanford, two franchise locations serving the Sandhills region. Running the business is where strategy meets payroll, dispatch, and customer complaints. It is also what qualifies her to advise other small business owners: she is working in the same chair they are.

Civic life and community leadership

Kristie sits on the board of the Cumberland County Economic Development Corporation, the Greater Fayetteville Chamber of Commerce, and the Cumberland County Partnership for Children. She serves as Marketing Chair for the Cool Spring Downtown Alliance and on an advisory committee for a local nonprofit. Earlier civic roles include serving as Secretary of the Fayetteville Home Builders Association and as Chamber liaison to the Fayetteville Young Professionals.

The through-line across all of it is the same work: getting people who do not normally sit at the same table to agree on what the community needs next, and then build it.
